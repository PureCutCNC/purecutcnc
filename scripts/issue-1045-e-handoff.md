## Design (replace the licensing approach; do not patch it)
**One standing-material model, owned by the guard.** For a square-blank program, the guard keeps a grid of the material still standing: along-bar bins × angle bins, each holding the material's outer radius, initialised from the raw square blank (corner envelope per angle; retained ends included). It walks **every move of the program in order, across operations**, and:
- for each move, samples the **actual commanded motion** (linear in along, A, Z; A as commanded, so 0→360 is a full turn, never a modulo shortcut) at steps no coarser than one grid bin in angle and along;
- at each sample computes the engagement against the current grid inside the cutter's footprint (its along extent ± radius, its angular extent at that radius), then lowers the grid to what the cutter leaves;
- refuses with `postRotarySquareTurnBelowEnvelope` (its message may be reworded to name the operation and say the cut takes more than the allowed depth) when a **turning cut** engages more than its operation's bound: the rough's `stepdown` (plus a stated tolerance); the finish's `stockToLeave` + the rough's scallop allowance (state it) — i.e. what is left after a rough that actually covered that spot;
- keeps refusing turning **rapids** below travel height, and along-mode turns below travel height, exactly as now;
- non-turning cuts lower the grid too (so an along rough clears material for a later around finish).
Grid resolution: state it (e.g. angle bins ≤ 1° and along bins ≤ a quarter of the smallest cutter radius in the program), keep memory and time bounded, report times on Frank-sized inputs (see Checks).
Then **delete `rotaryRoughBefore`** and the program-level licensing. The generator keeps a simple advisory: an around-mode finish on a square blank with no enabled rough before it in the same setup still generates but carries a warning ("rough first: the finish would cut into the square corners") — the guard is the authority at export. Say exactly what you changed in the generator's refusal and why.

**Angle bound, honest.** Make the around-mode interpolation bound a property of the **emitted motion**: between two consecutive cut samples in a ring the tool, moving linearly in A and Z, must not go below the independent safe tip by more than `tol`. `tol` = the larger of 0.005 mm (converted for inch) and the along path's own accepted between-sample error for the same operation (`s²/(8r)` for ball radius `r`, sample spacing `s`) — never worse than along mode. Achieve it by adaptive subdivision: where the midpoint check fails, sample the model at the mid angle (the sampler is per angle, so add angles to that ring only, or to all rings if simpler — state the cost) until it holds; keep the sagitta cap as the starting step. The hex test asserts the emitted moves (not ideal endpoints) against the independent reference with that `tol`.
