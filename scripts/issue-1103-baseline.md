# #1103 ca-a baseline (post-#1117)

Measured on the **unchanged** branch at `8fd5b18f`, whose base is
`integration/0.6.0` at `6498aaf5` (the #1117 merge). Nothing in this slice
changes the generator, so every number here is the post-#1117 output the later
slices compare against.

The fixtures are Frank's benchmark files copied into the ignored `work/`
directory (`busto-round-stock.camj`, `busto-rect-stock.camj`,
`square-to-round-test-01.camj`); they are never committed.

## Method

- **Protocol (machine-checked by `src/engine/toolpaths/rotaryBaseline.test.ts`):** warm-ups: 1, timed runs: 3, serial: true.
  One warm-up generation, then **three timed generations, serially**, and
  nothing generated in between. The numbers in `generation ms (runs)` are those
  three runs in order; `median` is their median. Only
  `computeOperationToolpath` is inside the timer — loading, posting and
  simulation are not. The timings were recaptured for the ca-a review's
  protocol correction: the first capture ran an extra, discarded generation
  after the warm-up, so those numbers were measured two generations warm.
  Every moves and posted hash is unchanged by the recapture.
- **Moves** are the emitted `ToolpathResult.moves` (all kinds); **rapids** are
  the `rapid` moves; **A reversals** are sign changes between consecutive
  **cutting** moves whose commanded A changes. A constant-A link, a rapid or a
  plunge is skipped, not counted as a reversal; the number counts transitions,
  so it is one fewer than the number of alternating rings in a clean program.
- The **posted-text hash** posts the same moves through the bundled `grbl`
  definition plus an own-drive rotary block about the stock's axis (the
  benchmark projects name no machine of their own), and normalises only the
  `Generated by PureCutCNC on <date>` line. The moves hash is SHA-256 of the
  emitted move stream (`JSON.stringify`), so it pins every point, kind and
  feed scale.
- `busto-round` and `busto-rect` name `rotaryPassDirection: 'around'`.
  `square-to-round` names none (a missing field reads as `'along'`), so its
  around rows are the same operations forced to `around` in memory; its
  `--along` rows are captured separately as the byte-identical control.

Single process at a time; no other benchmark or test pool ran beside these.

## Correction round 1 (review P1): shared height maps

The helix's phase grid is the ring grid's angle grid, so a map the ring grid
has already rasterised at a physical angle can serve the helix's row at that
same angle: the map depends only on the angle (mesh, tool and cell size are
fixed per operation) and the row still reads its own along positions.
`RotarySamplingWorkspace.angleMaps` now keeps those maps, cropped to the rows
(X bar) or columns (Y bar) the sampler reads, and the helix's row preps opt in
with `angleMaps: 'read-write'`. The ring grid itself is `'write'` and never
reads one back, so its rows -- and every move and post hash pinned above -- are
unchanged (a test compares them bit for bit). The cache is bounded: a map over
96 KiB is not stored, and the first map that would pass 32 MiB stops filling,
which keeps the earliest angles (the ring grid's, the ones the helix's first
round needs). Re-measured with the same protocol on the same machine:

- fixed cylinder finish: 360 -> 188 ms (2.01x -> 1.05x)
- fixed cylinder rough: 545 -> 197 ms (2.95x -> 1.07x)
- busto-round op0025 rough: 65534 -> 30772 ms (2.22x -> 1.04x)
- busto-round op0015 finish: 46019 -> 42220 ms (1.69x -> 1.55x)

The busto-round finish improves only in proportion to the helix rows that find
a different angle: the ring grid rasterises **3353** maps for that operation (404
base angles plus 2949 refinement angles), and each stored map is 61 KB -- a
17-row window over 400 columns, `data` plus the wall-coverage and model arrays --
so keeping all of them for the helix would cost about 205 MB. The 32 MiB budget
retains the first 548 and then stops filling (the policy keeps early entries
rather than evicting), and the helix's rows are spread over the whole circle, so
548 of its 2334 rows are reads and the rest re-rasterise angles the ring grid has
already built. The per-map gate is **not** what limits this: a 61 KB map is
under the 96 KiB gate, which is why the rows are stored at all.

Measured directly, by raising the budget to 400 MiB for one experiment: the same
operation then reuses 2070 of 2334 rows. The duplication is real, but its price
is ~125 MB of maps for the rows the helix actually asks for -- more memory than a
browser tab should hold for one operation, and not a trade this slice makes.
Bounding what a refused attempt may spend is correction round 2.

## Correction round 2 (review P1): a bounded attempt

The remaining cost was not the ring grid and not the shared-map gate; it was the
**refused helix attempt**. On this operation the attempt samples 2334 rows, 548
of them free reads and 1786 fresh rasters, at ~96 000 candidate triangles and
~7.6 ms per raster: ~171 M triangle passes, ~13.5 s of the 42.2 s generation,
all of it spent to prove that the band keeps its rings. The output was already
the ring fallback, so the work bought nothing.

`ContinuousHelixBudget` now carries a **work** limit next to its samples, rows
and rounds: `MAX_HELIX_RASTER_WORK` (24 M candidate triangles) is charged for
every row that rasterises a map and not for a reused one, and an attempt that
passes it reports `exhausted` with no points, exactly as the other three limits
do. The unit is triangles rather than milliseconds, so the same attempt obeys
the same limit on any machine; 24 M is about 2 s of raster at the ~80 ns per
candidate measured here, and ~160x the largest attempt any committed fixture
makes (150 k on the rotary fixture's around finish), so a helix that can verify
its band is not refused for cost. What it refuses is a mesh fine enough that
the speculation costs more than seconds, and the caller keeps the rings it has
already sampled -- the same motion an exhausted refinement produces.

Measured with the ca-e protocol (one warm-up, five timed serial generations, one
process at a time, both trees in the same session):

- busto-round op0015 generation: 42220 -> **29627 ms** median (1.55x -> **1.08x**
  against the 27558 ms baseline re-measured in the same session). The moves and
  their hash are **unchanged** (`976cbc22…`): the refused attempt was never part
  of the output.
- The other fixtures are untouched: every committed suite still pins the moves
  and posted hashes it carried, and the largest attempt measured while running
  them is 150 k candidate triangles, 160x under the cap. `busto-rect` and
  `square-to-round` are square blanks, which never attempt a helix, and
  `busto-round` op0025's rough helix attempt stays under the cap -- its moves
  hash is identical before and after (`980d7ced…`).

`rotaryBaseline.test.ts` now requires **every** generation row to be at or under
1.2x and requires the two tables' numbers to agree; there is no acceptance list
and no phrase that can stand in for one.

## ca-g: a refused band keeps the parts that fit

ca-g stops treating a helix band as all-or-nothing. When the attempt's refinement
is still unresolved on some segments after its eight rounds, the builder reports
the along spans of those segments, and `walkContinuousBand` splits the band there:
the ring positions covering each span (plus the ring on each side, so every helix
piece begins and ends on a **complete** ring) are cut as forward-only rings, and
each run between them gets its own helix attempt. Only a **tolerance** refusal
reports spans; a spent budget, cancellation, a pose with no model and a roughing
pose with no material at its layer still cost the band its helix. Every attempt of
one band is charged against that band's one `MAX_HELIX_RASTER_WORK`, so a split
cannot spend the speculation cap again per piece, and every seam is linked by the
caller's existing rule (#1093 on the finish, #1117 on the rough).

### Why op0015 and the single-deep-layer rough stay rings

Both are refused by the **work cap**, not by the tolerance one, so there are no
failing spans to split around -- and this is a measurement, not a reading of the
code. With `MAX_HELIX_RASTER_WORK` raised to 400 M for one experiment (nothing
committed), the same band on `busto-round op0015` reaches 8 rounds with 172 M
candidate triangles spent and 12 of 39664 segments still unresolved, after which
the split produces five pieces that cost another 227 M; the single-deep-layer
