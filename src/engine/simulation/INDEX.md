# INDEX — src/engine/simulation/

Material-removal simulation. Flat, round and square stock all use one radial
heightfield: `topZ` stores one height per cell and renders through a GPU
heightfield mesh. A square blank is the round bar's heightfield whose cells
start at the blank's true cross section; nothing downstream branches on stock
shape. Pure logic + Three.js mesh builders — no React.

## Core
- `aroundWrap.ts` — compile-time Around shader chunks: shared wrap and radial-normal formulas, periodic neighbor lookup and render bounds; `aroundBounds` is centred on the true pivot and defaults its maximum to the grid's radial envelope (`stockTopZ`), so a square blank's corners fit at `pivot ± side/√2` rather than a lifted `0..2·maxRadius` while a round bar stays byte-identical. The neighbour lookup is a preprocessor-free integer chunk (`AROUND_CELL_INDEX_GLSL`) so the scalar test support can run the GPU's own code; flat materials retain their original shaders
- `types.ts` — `SimulationGrid`, `DirtyRegion`, `SimulationBuildOptions`, `SimulationStats`; `stockTopZ` is the grid's enclosing radial maximum (the bar radius, or a square blank's corner radius) and `initialTopZ` is an optional immutable per-cell starting baseline used only by removal statistics. `SquareBarDescriptor` is initialization input; the grid carries no runtime stock-shape discriminator
- `grid.ts` — grid spec resolution and `createSimulationGrid` allocation. A square blank enters only here: `validateSquareBar` checks the descriptor, `seedSquareTopZ` fills each across cell with its starting radius `halfSide / max(|sin θ|, |cos θ|)` (constant along the bar), and `createSimulationGrid` snapshots the result as `initialTopZ`. `stockTopZ` is the corner envelope (`side/√2`); the true pivot stays `frame.radius`
- `tools.ts` — `cutterSurfaceZ`: the cutter's lower-surface profile used to lower flat-grid cells; `cutterRayInterval` (and its allocation-free `cutterRayIntervalInPlace`) is the wrapped counterpart: the exact radial span the cutter's solid removes on one ray, solved in closed form for the flat cylinder, the ball nose plus shank, and the V-bit cone plus shank
- `replay.ts` — the one cutting path for every stock shape. A flat grid and the plasma torch keep the flat-sheet kernel byte-for-byte (a torch cuts a flat one-kerf, full-depth slot along every torch-on move and a kerf-wide hole at each pierce). A wrapped (Around) grid dispatches to a second kernel that resolves each cell against the cutter's true solid (issue #1016 C2-F8): the unrolled across distance is arc length at the frame radius `R0 = period/2π`, so a cell at radius `r` and angle `D` sits `r·sin D` across the cutter and `r·cos D` along it, not flat. The cell keeps one radius, so only a cutter that reaches the outside lowers it; one that passes through the ray's middle leaves the outer shell. Its across bounding box uses the tight profile-specific reach (flat `R0·atan(rt/tip)`, ball `R0·asin(rt/(tip+rt))`, V-bit at its truncation) and cells at or below the move's deepest tip are skipped before the trig; every cell in the applied piece's footprint is resolved against all periodic images and their ray intervals are iterated to the order-independent fixed point (F8b), so overlapping seam copies cannot make finished playback differ from a batch replay and a cell the cutter already contains is removed in that frame; a move whose Z changes by more than one cell across the cutter's diameter is **swept** at one fixed set of absolute sample poses half a cell of Z apart plus each cell's nearest pose (`wrappedNeedsSweep`/`wrappedSweepSampleFractions`), because the disk edge reaches a cell before or after its closest approach and on an ascending move that earlier pose is lower (issue #1048 review); a playback piece applies exactly the samples with `t <= elapsed` (plus the nearest pose once the piece has reached it) and never adds the reached endpoint as an extra pose, so the union over any sequence of pieces is the batch and the finish equals it for every step size. Each cell evaluates only the sample poses whose cutter footprint can reach its current top — the along offset within `rt`, and the across reach `R0·asin(sqrt(rt² - along²)/top)` — widening the range as the fixed point lowers the top, so a circumferential ramp costs the cutter's footprint, not the move's sample count. A horizontal (or sub-cell-ramp) move keeps the exact nearest-pose path clamped to `min(t, elapsed)`, with the batch as a ceiling so a thread's next turn or a ring's far seam image cannot cut a slot the cutter has not reached. Statistics compare each cell with its own `initialTopZ` baseline when one exists (removed count, minimum top and maximum removed depth) and keep the uniform `stockTopZ` comparison for flat/round; the cell loop is allocation-free with per-move cutter dispatch (see `replay.test.ts` for the flat-sheet reference-parity contract)
- `playback.ts` — playback state: poses, options, grid cloning for stepped playback. Forward seeks advance incrementally (cuts are monotonic); the dirty region accumulates until the caller uploads and clears it; `cloneSimulationGrid` copies the mutable `topZ` and shares the immutable `initialTopZ` by reference, and `reset` restores the prior-operation base grid's heights. Each subdivided piece remembers the source move it came from and hands it to `applyMoveToGrid` as the `sweep` together with the fraction of it the piece has reached (`elapsedFractionOf`); the piece bounds only the elapsed footprint, so a partial application cuts what the cutter has actually reached (issue #1048) and the fixed point makes repeated pieces idempotent (issue #1016 C2-F8/F8b); a forward seek that lands within a rounding step of the end finishes the path, so `seekToFraction(1)` leaves `isFinished()` true

## Rendering (Three.js)
- `gpuMesh.ts` — builds a `DataTexture` over the grid's `topZ` array and an instanced row-strip surface with one flat top per cell (no shared corners that can collapse narrow tab bridges); bounds cover the shader-displaced Y range through `aroundBounds` and the grid's radial envelope, so a square blank's corners are not clipped; `uploadHeightfieldRegion` pushes only the dirty rectangle to the GPU via `texSubImage2D`
- `instancedBoundary.ts` — boundary walls + stock underside as instanced row-strips whose geometry the vertex shader derives from `gl_InstanceID` + the heightfield texture (GLSL3 `texelFetch`); O(cols) template memory at any detail, no CPU rebuilds while cutting. The one heightfield boundary path for flat, round and square static and playback views, with the same radial-envelope bounds. A step gets a wall lit by its own normal; a riser inside a slope is drawn too, but lit as the surface it belongs to
- `heightfieldShader.ts` — the heightfield surface shader material (`createHeightfieldMaterial`) plus the GLSL both meshes share: the `LIGHTING_GLSL` light rig, the `STEP_GLSL` step/slope predicate (`edgeIsStep`), and the smoothed surface normal (`SLOPE_GRADIENT_GLSL` + `SURFACE_NORMAL_GLSL`). The surface takes a lighting gradient only across an edge `edgeIsStep` calls a slope, interpolated across the cell so it is continuous over cell borders and widened when a cell is under a pixel; `instancedBoundary.ts` lights the steps it leaves flat, so the two meshes cannot disagree about which of them shades an edge
- `toolMesh.ts` — builds/disposes the moving cutter mesh group; a plasma torch gets the same plain flat-endmill cylinder as a flat endmill

## Tests
- `aroundReplay.test.ts` — multi-turn folded replay, periodic cutter overlap on both seam sides and axes, dirty-region union, flat-reference parity and playback/scrub parity
- `aroundWrap.test.ts` — executes the actual scalar GLSL against the frozen rotary transforms on both axes in every quadrant; tests bar-group turn/tool placement, radial normals, cylinder bounds, the square corner envelope about the true pivot and compile-time material variants; also runs the seam's integer `aroundCell` lookup (both bar axes, along index never wrapped) and the `sameAroundFrame` comparison that keys the viewport's effects (issue #395 G3)
- `gpuMesh.test.ts` — heightfield texture/mesh construction and per-cell top geometry
- `tabbedEdgeSimulation.test.ts` — real rectangle/Edge Out/auto-tab toolpath replay at two detail levels, including playback and through-cut neighbors
- `instancedBoundary.test.ts` — strip template scaling, instance counts, group wiring, and the step predicate and surface normal the surface and walls share
- `heightfieldShader.test.ts` — the step/slope predicate and the lighting gradient's scalar helpers, run from the GLSL source: a ball scallop at ~3 cells per pass is a slope, tab bridges and cut-through rims stay steps, a step at or below half a cell is a slope, the gradient is continuous across cell borders and takes nothing from a step. The texture lookups and wiring around those helpers are restated in the test; the assembled shaders are covered by the rendered checks in `e2e/viewportViews.smoke.spec.ts`
- `glslScalar.testSupport.ts` — test support that compiles the shaders' scalar GLSL functions to JavaScript, so those tests call the text the GPU compiles rather than a TypeScript copy. `ivec2` is a plain `{ x, y }` and `int`/`ceil` are builtins, with function-read uniforms passed as `globals`, so integer code like `aroundCell` runs too (issue #395 G3)
- `replay.test.ts` — optimized flat cut-kernel parity against the `cutterSurfaceZ` reference; the wrapped seam as a pure one-period translation (the old flat-sheet parity moved on purpose with F8) and the guarantee that flat/round grids gain no square keys
- `wrappedPlayback.test.ts` — a part-played wrapped move removes only what the cutter has reached (issue #1048): a hand-built three-turn thread with flat/ball/V-bit cutters, a Flutes-like twisted line, an around-the-bar ring, ascending and descending Z ramps (including the reviewer's 0.2-mm, k = 197 case and the 1.752-vs-2.109 ascending cell), plunges and a seam-crossing ramp are compared frame by frame with a batch replay of the path truncated at the playback fraction; every fraction check is symmetric (both directions within one cell) and asserts the playback itself removed cells, and the finish must equal the full batch for step sizes `totalPathLength/k`, `k ∈ {7, 53, 197, 1000}`, and for forward/backward seek sequences, not only a direct seek to 100 %. Frank's fixture at 2 % and 6 % uses the same symmetric check. Mutations: restoring the reached pose as an extra sample fails `steps/7`, and restoring the nearest-pose ceiling fails the ascending ramp at 10 %
- `playback.test.ts` — playback move preparation, feed scaling, seek parity and dirty-region accumulation; plus the round clone key contract and wrapped round playback-versus-batch parity
- `wrappedCutterGeometry.test.ts` — the wrapped cutter's true width (issues #1016 C2-F8/F8b): random moves on round and square grids, both bar axes and all three profiles, checked cell by cell against a dense physical oracle that tests points against the cutter's real solid (ramp moves are swept over the kernel's absolute sample poses plus the nearest pose, the #1048-review batch fix); a ray through the cutter's middle keeps the outer shell; the ball shank carries a ray the nose cannot; the 25 mm square-to-round case lowers zero retained-end cells; a round-bar groove matches the oracle through its walls; seam copies, playback batch == subdivided and reset; the reviewer's overlapping-seam move finishes playback exactly equal to batch under default, fine and unsplit pieces; a partial playback cuts the cell at (5, 0) at X = 4.5 while leaving a cell beyond the elapsed footprint alone; the 25 mm ball replay stays inside its CPU-time bound at detail 280 and 1120 on the program's constant-Z passes (the full flat reach mutation trips the 1120 bound), and the reviewer's circumferential ramp at detail 1120 stays inside a 50x ratio against the same XY move without a Z change, which never evaluates a sample (scanning every sample per cell trips it at 613x)
- `squareGrid.test.ts` — square grid initialization: the true cross section seeded at every across cell, the immutable baseline, the corner render envelope with the pivot at side/2, virgin zero stats, old-grid key identity and descriptor validation
- `squareReplay.test.ts` — the square blank through the shared kernel and playback: corner cells above the inscribed radius lower monotonically and never rise, a tool above the envelope removes nothing, angle-changing moves follow round behavior, a wrapped square grid uses the round seam copies, and batch/playback/subdivision/seek/reset agree. Statistics measure each cell's own baseline (a virgin square reports zero removed), and the actual generated rough keeps untouched ends square while the actual finish replays and seeks
