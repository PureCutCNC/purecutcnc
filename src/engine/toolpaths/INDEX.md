# INDEX — src/engine/toolpaths/

Toolpath generators. Each file owns one strategy. `index.ts` re-exports everything.

## Rotary model sampling
- `rotaryRaster.ts` — the fused rotary projection and height-map raster used by around mode (`buildRotaryHeightMap`, `buildRotaryRasterGeometry`, issue #1045 slice T). Per-triangle contiguous `(across, up)` / bar-axis records avoid chasing vertex indices; the bar-axis cell range is angle-free and cached per cell size. Every triangle that reaches the strip is considered, and none is dropped: since #1050 slice B a triangle that covers no cell centre raises a cell only where its clipped maximum is a local peak over that cell and its eight neighbours (more than 1 % of a cell above all of them); slice C collects such a triangle without projecting it in the centre pass and rejects it in the clip pass with one comparison per overlapped cell against a precomputed 3x3 neighbourhood max, so only the survivors are projected and clipped. The kernel builds the exact same map as `buildHeightMap` over a turned mesh on both axes and both projection paths, including the peak rule, the clipped maximum and its window agreement.
- `rotaryRaster.test.ts` — exact map and tip-row parity against `buildHeightMap` on both bar axes, with full/row-only/column-only windowed maps, triangle subsets, `trackCellRise`, and several cell sizes. Its fixture includes thin no-centre triangles, so the #1050 clip pass and the peak rule are covered on both kernels.
- `thinFeatureHeightMap.test.ts` — #1050: a 0.02 mm fin and a long smooth sliver, both missing every cell centre. Pins the 1 %-of-a-cell local-peak threshold (strict, and one high neighbour disqualifies a peak), that a smooth steep (87-degree) triangulated ramp does not trigger the rule at all, that a wall's face beside a floor does not raise the floor cell on either kernel (the neighbour already reads the wall's top), the clipped maximum (the mutation that uses the triangle's global maximum over-raises by three cells and fails), that a clipped triangle adds no `cellRise` (its value is already exact inside the cell), and the reproductions: Codex's 0.02 x 10 mm fin on a closed extruded base (the around finish at A0 must reach the 10 mm top, not the base), the #1095 tiny blank's 1 mm tube (the along rough must not cut into it; around meets model + stock to leave), and the same fin through the flat parallel finish.
- `rotaryModel.ts` — slice C adds `rotaryAlongRange` (end supports, chuck-end detection, cutter-radius clamp clearance, intersected with the model to preserve attachment) and `generateRotaryRough` (one shared target sampling, radial layers clamped to final tips, air skipped in separate runs, travel between runs at the bar's surface plus a clearance (`safeRadius` only for the operation's first positioning move and its final retract), finish-style end links and unbounded angles across layers); empty ranges emit `rotaryNoRoom`. Also owns per-angle model turning through the frozen rotary frame and centreline tool-tip radius sampling, with fresh orientation cache hosts, progress, stopping, and deduplicated height-map warnings (issue #1015 slice A); plus `generateRotaryFinish` (slice B), the rotary finishing pass: `ceil(2 pi R / stepover)` evenly spaced angles, samples along the bar, zigzag passes whose links turn at the same along position at the bar's surface plus a 1 mm clearance, never above the retract height (one move each for the rise, turn and drop where they are non-zero), stock to leave applied by enlarging the sampling tool and adding it back at the tip so a ball's offset is normal to the surface (exact on a convex contact's vertical normal, and the reason the answer differs from a plain vertical offset in a valley), `NaN` (no model under the tool) not cut in the finish -- each pass is cut in runs of finite samples, rising to the bar's surface plus a clearance between runs and between passes that do not join at the same bar end (only the opening positioning move and the closing retract go to `safeRadius`), and a pass with no finite sample emitting nothing -- while the rough reads a `NaN` as the centreline; below-zero clamped to the centreline with one `rotaryBelowCentreline` warning naming the first place (the value that decides it carries the stock offset, so leaving stock does not hide a below-centreline cut), values above `R` clamped to the surface, an opening positioning rapid over the first sample at `safeRadius` before the plunge, ball and flat end mills only (`rotaryToolUnsupported` otherwise), one `rotaryModelOutsideBar` warning when any mesh vertex lies outside the bar (farther from its axis than `R`, or past an end, with the 3D view's minimum render height as tolerance), a refusal of a non-positive or non-finite `stepover` or `sampleSpacing` or of a bar with no positive radius (otherwise the sample counts are unbounded and the worker hangs), the retained bar beyond the cut range added to the mesh that is sampled as two closed coaxial cylinders of the bar's radius, one from each bar end to the cut range, triangulated so their facets undercut the true surface by no more than the view's minimum render height (the existing tool-shape sampling then keeps the cutter's side out of the bar that holds the part, while the outside-bar check still judges the model alone), and per-pass collinear-point merging that never joins a run across a move that turns the stock or reverses along the bar and is Douglas--Peucker -- a segment replaces a run only when every point of it is within the tolerance of that segment, so a curved profile cannot collapse into one chord below the surface. A return (odd) pass reads its height with the same along index its position uses, not the mirrored row. Points are the wrapped Around form (`{x, y, z, a}` with the tool over the centreline), so export, the 3D view and the simulation read them unchanged. Slice I makes travel between runs always the bar's surface plus the clearance -- never capped by `safeRadius`, which the operation's first positioning move and final retract go to the higher of -- and splits a step steeper than the sample spacing in a run (the retained bar at a cut range end, or a steep model wall) into a vertical leg plus a horizontal leg at the higher tip instead of one diagonal that interpolates through the corner. Slice J inlines `toolSpacePoint`'s rotation in `turnModelInto`: cos/sin/centre/half-height once per angle, the turned coordinates written straight into a Float32Array with the same arithmetic order as the frame function, no per-vertex point object. The turned X/Y bounds are folded into that same pass and rounded to Float32, so they equal `computeXYBounds(out)` exactly and the separate per-angle scan is gone; `sampleRotaryModel` reuses one turned buffer across all angles instead of allocating a fresh 3 V array per angle. On the 1.47 M-vertex bust (scratch `work/rotaryBench.ts`) turning fell from ~860 ms to ~29 ms an angle and one Rotary rough / finish from ~361 s / ~384 s to ~14 s each, with byte-identical move arrays. The retained-bar stubs, the `modelOutsideBar` check and the mesh concatenation already ran once per operation and still do; the per-angle height map (~48 ms/angle) is now the dominant remaining cost. #1016 C2-S3 widens every entry point to `BarStock` and adds the square blank: the pivot is `barPivotZ = side / 2` (never the corner radius), the angular step and the tip cap use `barMaxRadius = side / √2` (conservative; identical to the round radius when the bar is round), a rough layer's band is counted from `barMaxRadius` but a pass skips the bands above its own material (C2-F2), travel between runs is `pivot + barMaxRadius + 1 mm` (or the higher requested `safeRadius`), model containment is the physical box (`|across − centre| ≤ side / 2` and `|up − pivot| ≤ side / 2`) rather than a circle, retained material beyond the cut range is a closed square prism per end, and the ray `barSurfaceRadius` is never used as finite-cutter support. Round output is byte-identical: the shared helpers return exactly the old values on a round bar. The C2-S3 correction adds the coarse-spacing shoulder safety: on a square blank the along grid is refined at both cut-range ends — sampled at the height map's own cell resolution across one cutter footprint plus that cell — because a retained stub puts a *step* in the required tip height one footprint inside the range, and a straight move between two coarse samples cuts the chord below it (the 45° manager repro dropped the cutter's rim 0.490904 mm into the retained square while the steep-step guard stayed silent at a 12 mm spacing). There the step either makes the rise exceed the spacing so `emitRotaryRuns` emits its stepped move, or the residual chord dip is at most one cell. Round bars and the rest of the range are untouched, and the derived pass/interval counts are now bounded against a sample budget so a positive-but-tiny `sampleSpacing` refuses with `operationStepoverRatioRange` instead of allocating an unbounded grid (#1016 C2-S3 correction). That budget is checked against the **refined** along count, not only the uniform `passes × (intervals + 1)` grid: the two shoulder zones add up to `2 * MAX_SHOULDER_SAMPLES` positions, so a 100,000-pass job with a tiny finite cell (300,000 uniform samples, ~8.2e8 refined) refuses before an angle row is allocated or an angle is sampled. And when `MAX_SHOULDER_SAMPLES` would widen the added spacing past the **narrowest** effective height-map cell (the flat-aligned one; the corner-aligned cell is up to `2^(1/4)` wider), the refinement's one-cell proof cannot hold, so the job refuses rather than accepting a coarser tolerance silently. The C2 correction M1 validates the radial allowance before sampling (`finite`, `>= 0`, and the derived sampling radius `> 0`) and refuses with `rotaryStockToLeaveInvalid` and no motion; `sampleRotaryModel` reports invalid tool geometry as `valid: false` with no angle rows, so it can never be read as the legitimate "no model under the tool" `NaN` and become a cut to the axis. The C2-F2 correction stops the square rough starting every pass at the corner: each pass computes `squareRotaryTouchRadius` and emits the layer only when `layerRadius` is below the blank material the cutter can reach inside its cross-sectional slab, so the flat passes no longer spend their first layers in air. The C2-F2b correction fixes that bound's geometry: a flat cutter's bottom is a disc at the tip, so the material that matters is the blank cross-section's height inside the slab `|x| <= rt`, not the radial distance `r(phi)` over an angular window; the old bound emitted air passes at `Z = 20.1421`/`20.0421` above a 20 mm flat. The C2-F2c correction makes the ball's gate exact: the call passes the tool type, and for a ball the helper returns `max(Y − rt + sqrt(rt² − X²))` over the square inside the slab — the ball's disc (centre at `tip + rt`) plus the shank above it — instead of the flat cylinder bound, which is only an upper bound and kept whole air layers (20 mm square, 4 mm ball, A30: `Z = 22.642136` versus the true contact `Z = 21.856406`). The maximum is a rotated vertex, an edge crossing of `x = ±rt`, or the interior tangency `X* = m rt / sqrt(1 + m²)` on an edge (`g` is concave), so it is still closed form with no sampling. Round bars short-circuit the gate and their output is unchanged byte for byte. The C2-F6 correction floors every finite sample's tip at the exact retained-stub contact (`squareRotaryStubContactFloor`) after the normal stock offset is folded back in, and `retainedStubStepForced` splits a segment whose straight chord would dip below that floor (the contact height falls steeply just outside a square corner, so a 0.7 mm drop over the 0.733 mm sample spacing sagged 0.12 mm into the stub when the samples were merely floored); both are square-only, shared by the rough and the finish, and a round bar never calls either. The C2-F6b correction applies that floor to the rough's `NaN` fallback as well: a `NaN` sample still means "do not cut" to the finish, but the rough's "cut to the axis" can drop the cutter's side into a stub when an off-axis target leaves whole pass rows with no model under the tool, so the rough's effective tip is `max(axis, stubFloor)` per sample (`stubFloor` is `-Infinity` where no stub reaches, leaving the old fallback byte for byte; round bars are untouched). #1016 C2-F7 pulls the cutter centre one radius inside a square blank's stubbed range ends (`rotaryCutterSpan`; `params.alongStart`/`alongEnd` stay the band and the stub faces): every sample is at or beyond the exact F6 floor's reach, so the floor stops binding, a pass is a rapid at the travel height, a straight plunge, one feed and a straight retract, and the band within one cutter radius of a stub keeps the shoulder fillet. A span that collapses refuses with `rotaryNoRoom`; round bars and an end that meets the bar's own end are unchanged. #1045 slice A adds `RotaryFinishParams.passDirection`: missing or `'along'` is the motion above, byte for byte (a two-argument `Math.hypot` still measures a run's spacing, and the along grid, angle list and emission are untouched), while `'around'` swaps the two roles -- one ring per along position (`stepover` apart, `boundedIntervals`) and an angle every `sampleSpacing` of surface distance round `barMaxRadius`'s circumference, both 0 and 360 present so a ring closes, with the product of the two counts under `MAX_ROTARY_SAMPLES` (the refusal now checks the mode's own counts). Rings are visited in along order and alternate direction (a single counter continues across the rough's layers) so A stays in [0, 360] and each ring starts where the last ended; the finish keeps its `NaN` = not cut rule and the rough its per-sample `needsCut`/layer/`stubFloor` rules, and every point reads its own `(k, i)` -- a reversed ring uses its reversed `k` for tip, angle and along alike. `emitRotaryRuns` gains an optional `pivot`: when present it adds the arc `rMin * |dA| * pi/180` to the steep-step spacing, so an in-ring rise is compared with the surface distance rather than the zero X/Y distance, and the along pass (which never passes it) is unchanged. A **square** blank supports around mode (#1045 slice C): the slice-A square refusal and its warning code are gone, and each ring sample is cut only when its layer radius is below `squareRotaryTouchRadius` at **that sample's own angle** (the along pass's air skip, per angle), so no layer is spent above a flat. A sample can never take more than one `stepdown` out of the material standing at its angle: a layer is exactly one `stepdown` below the layer above, and the layer above either cut there (one stepdown lower) or skipped the sample because its material was already below its radius. The retained-stub contact floor already applied by `rotaryTargets` stays per (k, i), and the C2-F7 cutter-centre pull-in keeps the cutter body a radius off each stub face; rings are separate runs, so no cut chord spans two ring positions and the along shoulder refinement is not needed in this mode. An around **finish** only follows the model (2026-10-22 decision): no clearing layers and no link to a rough, on a square or a round bar alike. It therefore has no layer depth, no emission budget and no stepdown refusal; a finish-only program cuts to full depth in one pass and the adapter (`rotaryOperation.ts`) carries the advisory `rotaryFinishWithoutRough` when no enabled rough on the same model precedes it in the same setup. The slice-K/L licensing (`squareRoughLeft`, `squareRoughIds`, `rotarySquareFinishNeedsRough`, `postSquareAroundRoughMissing`) stays gone: a bare finish, a disabled or later rough, and an along rough over a retained end all generate the same finish motion. `motionPipeline.ts` admits a turning **cut** below `rotaryTravelZ` only by regenerating the around operation and requiring the posted motion to equal it **exactly** (`postSquareAroundToolpathStale`); a rapid turn and an along-mode turn still refuse. A round bar is unchanged: its blank is already the turned surface's envelope, so the finish's model-following ring is the same single pass it always was. #1045 slice F also makes the around sampler's emitted motion hold its stated bound: with `conservativeCellBounds` (around mode only) the sampler raises every sample by the largest cell-centre rise the operation's own rasterised triangles need — `(|dz/dx| + |dz/dy|) * cellSize / 2`, capped at one cell — so the emitted tip stands at or above the model, and it queries the tool's support from the cell's nearest point so a ball's lift is not under-read either. The pad is one scalar per angle, not per cell: a per-cell margin would make neighbouring samples differ by it, which the around refinement reads as a cusp and subdivides every segment for. The between-sample tolerance is then the only remaining error. Round and along output are untouched **except** as #1045 slice D changes them: the C2-F7 cutter-centre pull-in now applies to a **round bar's** stubbed ends too (`rotaryCutterSpan` no longer gates on `isSquareStock`), because a ring or a pass on the band edge had the cutter body inside the retained end by an overlap thinner than a height-map cell and cut into it (the review's Ø40 x 60 bar, band 18..42, Ø6 flat, 2.7 stepover, 0.75 sample spacing: the ring at 20.6667 overlapped by 0.3333 mm and cut at Z24.97 against the stub's Z40 surface; along mode gouged at the same position for 2.9 and 0.5 sample spacings, so both modes share the fix, and a band that ends inside the bar now starts and ends one cutter radius in). It also caps the around angle step at `barMaxRadius * dTheta^2 / 8 <= 0.005 mm` (converted from mm into the operation's units) on top of the `sampleSpacing` arc step, because a safe pair of angular endpoints does not make the linear A interpolation between them safe on a convex section: on the radius-8 hex prism with the 1.5 mm ball the uncapped A325.7143 -> 330 move gouged 0.0396 mm at its midpoint, and the cap takes the step from 4.2857 to 2.5532 degrees and the sweep error to 0.0141 mm. Both the cap and the round pull are reflected by the re-captured fixtures below. Along finishes likewise only follow the model: the same 2026-10-22 decision removed the along clearing and the finish's `stepdown` field. A finish-only along program cuts to full depth in one pass and carries the advisory; a finish after a rough emits exactly the base commit's motion except where #1045 slice D's retained-end pull-in applies. `ToolpathResult` carries no `roughLeft`, the adapter regenerates no preceding rough, and along ROUGH output is byte-identical. The finish's only addition is the advisory warning, and #1045 slice V makes the cache model its input: `rotaryPrecedingRoughs` captures the enabled rotary roughs before the finish in the same setup and the STL models each targets, so enabling, disabling, adding, removing, reordering or moving one, or retargeting it onto another model, invalidates only that finish's entry; the warning itself is per targeted model, so a rough on B does not protect A. #1045 slice T (performance, move output byte-identical) gives one operation one `RotarySamplingWorkspace`: the cross-section, hull, strip plan and candidate buffer are built once and reused by every around refinement round; the refinement caches each sampled midpoint tip row and samples only angles it has not seen, instead of re-rasterising every segment each round; and `buildStripPlan` no longer excludes triangles that cover no axial cell centre: #1050 may clip those to the cells they overlap when they are a local peak, so dropping them would make the fused kernel disagree with the general one on exactly the thin features the clip exists for. On the manager's bust fixtures this takes around mode from ~127 s / ~78 s to ~6 s / ~5 s and the along rough from ~13 s to ~3.5 s in the same probe, with bit-identical move arrays on all four runs. #1093 links consecutive around rings with a feed along the bar at the angle their runs share: the finish follows the model through a dense sample row, the rough rises only to the material its own earlier layers left plus the 1 mm clearance, the first layer keeps the travel height, an A-changing link stays at the travel height, and a split ring, a gap in the reachable surface, an angle change or an over-budget row falls back to the old retract/travel path. #1096 makes both generators region-aware: `RotaryFinishParams.region` is the Around-sheet mask, every sample outside it is dropped before it can become a cut (so a ring or pass splits into the runs that are inside, and the existing emitter links or retracts between them), and the finish's #1093 ring-to-ring surface link additionally requires every intermediate point to be inside the region -- a link that would feed across excluded area falls back to the non-cutting retract/traverse/plunge. Slice D makes a region-clipped around finish ring link like a whole one: a ring the region trims to one contiguous interior arc, with no model gap inside it, counts as reaching both of its ends, so the #1093 surface link feeds to the reversed next ring at the shared boundary angle instead of retracting and plunging on every ring; `followLink`'s exact-angle and inside-region checks still gate it, so a boundary that moves between rings falls back. A ring cut into several angle pieces, a ring with a model gap inside the region, and a region whose arc wraps the A0/A360 seam (it arrives as two pieces, one at each end of the ring) keep the old retract. Along mode has no #1093 surface link at all and is unchanged. `rotaryRegionContains` maps one tool position `(along, a)` to the sheet as `centre + a * pi * thickness / 360` on the bar's across axis, the same convention `aroundFrame.ts` uses; the mask itself is built periodic by `regions.ts`. A square blank's A turns outside the region keep the existing travel-height rule. Absent/null region leaves every number and branch above untouched.

- `finishSurface.ts` — 3D finish pass dispatcher, home to `criticalWaterlineFloorZs`, and since #711 to `hasMachinableSurface`, the one place that decides whether a height-map pass has anything to cut at an XY. - `finishSurfaceConstantScallop.ts` — constant-scallop finish strategy (issue #705, phase 2 of #697). The adaptive refinement's own budget is priced before it is spent (issue #698): it used to be a single global ring counter of 1,000 enforced as rings were emitted, and since bands are processed from the top of the model down that made it first-come-first-served — whatever was highest got refined and everything below it got nothing. **A finer Adaptive spacing therefore produced a worse surface**, because halving the spacing doubled what each band consumed and the counter ran out higher up: flat area never cut on the hills fixture ran 14.3 / 14.3 / 16.2 / 33.9 % at 1.20 / 0.60 / 0.30 / 0.15 mm, and at 0.15 mm was no better than switching adaptive refinement off. - `xyLead.ts` — tangent-arc lead-in/lead-out (issue #695). The rule is not the pass flag but **does this pass leave a surface that survives**: a plunge on a floor is axial into material about to be removed, while a plunge on a wall puts the full flute radially against a surface that stays, at zero XY feed, and rubs a witness line down it. So a context is handed only to wall-defining contours — the finish pass's wall and island-wall contours, and a roughing pass's root ring when `stockToLeaveRadial` is zero (`roughingRingIsTheFinishedWall`) — and only a FULL entry is retargeted (`transitionLinksAtDepth` in `pocket.ts` decides; a contour reached by an at-depth link is already engaged). The lead is a SINGLE arc tangent at the join, searched radius-major (1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25 x D) because radius sets how fast the cutter engages (the gap at arc-distance d is about d^2/2R); sweep only breaks ties. The join is a POINT, not a vertex index — a four-vertex wall contour is all corners, and arriving at a corner means coming from outside the domain — so the plan carries the re-seamed contour. Turn side is predicted by a probe either side and proven by the domain gate. A lead may touch exactly ONE surface, the one it joins: `buildOffsetDomainCheck` admits boundary points on purpose (the ring paths ride them), so beyond `sqrt(2R x margin)` from the join — where the arc has curved clear by the margin on its own — validation switches to `buildClearanceCheck`, the same domain held `entryBoundarySafety` off every wall. Without it the arc crosses a channel and comes to rest against the wall opposite, arriving perpendicular to it; on a crowded pocket that put an entry's staging point, and both exits' far ends, at zero clearance from a wall they were not joining — the plunge back on a finished surface. The exit is emitted PER CONTOUR, immediately after it is cut: once per level left every wall but the last being departed by stopping and travelling away, which rubs the surface exactly as the plunge did. Feed is ONE constant `min(plungeFeed / feed, 1)`: a per-move ramp splits the arc-fitting run and linearises the arc. Shapes come from `buildTangentLeadPath` and the domain gate from `tangentLink.ts`, at half its chord budget so leads sample between vertices. Edge routes carry it too, on both sides, but only where the descent at the staging point is RAMPED (`descentCanAffordALead`, and see `edge.ts`): a rough edge route clears a channel of exactly `2r`, so staging a lead off the wall moves the descent into virgin stock, and only a helix or ramp makes full radial engagement there ordinary slotting rather than a plunge — asking for a lead with a plunging entry is declined with `xyLeadNeedsRampedEntry` and is byte-identical to not asking. A finish edge route additionally ARRIVES on an arc but LEAVES without one (`XyLeadContext.exitArc`, #708): the two ends are not symmetric, because the arrival has a helix under it — `synthesizeEntry` bores the staging point full-diameter before the arc reaches it — while the departure drives out of the finished slot into whatever stands beside it and then retracts vertically out of the blind pocket it just cut. One stepdown deep that is ordinary slotting, which is why a ROUGHING pass keeps its exit; on a finish pass it is the whole depth at once. Measured on `work/edge-in-test-exit-cut.camj` (1/4 in cutter, 0.75 in stock, roughing leaving 0.040 in): the cleared band runs 0.040 to 0.290 in out from the wall and the finish exit arc drove to 0.500 in, 0.21 in into untouched slug at full depth. A narrower arc does not rescue it — the room a finish pass has is exactly the radial stock roughing left, and the narrowest rung of the ladder (0.25 x D) is already wider than that. A clearing pass is unaffected: its exit sweeps into the cavity its own rings emptied. `roughingRingIsTheFinishedWall` is also what the CAM panel's row visibility asks (#708): the control used to be offered on any roughing pass whose pattern cuts rings, ungated on `stockToLeaveRadial` so that changing the stock could not make it vanish — but the user could then pick it and watch the generator quietly ignore it, because stock left means a finish pass machines the mark away. Offering a setting that cannot change the program is the worse of the two, and the stored value survives the row disappearing. An inside route shares the pocket's cavity domain, while an OUTSIDE route has no cavity to sweep into, so `domainOutsideLoops` builds the domain the other way up — everything outside the retained part and the other features standing in the Z span, all grown to tool-centre distance, inside a box that is only there to bound the test. `withKeepOut` then subtracts the tab footprints standing at each level, per level because the tab pass runs after generation and only lifts vertical moves and splits cuts, so a lead planned through a tab would drive into it with nothing downstream to correct it. Trochoidal edge roughing is excluded on measured grounds, not by omission: it enters through its own helix away from the wall and reaches the wall by widening orbits, so the fixture that plunges onto the wall at every level as a contour route produces no such descent at all as a trochoidal one. Corner relief is deliberately NOT led, on measurement: its 12-of-12 descents do land on the tool-centre wall contour, but it descends at `P` on purpose — `mainPathCutsAt` proves that column was already cut, the contact there is tangent so engagement is zero, and the `P -> E` excursion that follows is itself the progressive ramp a lead would supply. A region mask, a raster pattern, or nothing viable falls back to the direct entry with a structured warning

Both the arc and the return are sampled against the Pocket tool-centre domain; a corner failing either keeps its exact sharp geometry, so one reflex corner (whose arc always bulges off the ring into the wall) costs only itself and not the whole ring. `cleanupCount` reports how many corners were actually cleaned — 0 with planned transitions is what raises `pocketWallCornerCleanupFallback`.
- `seedClearing.ts` — seeded circle pocket clearing, phase 1 (issue #554), reached by `pocketPattern: 'seeded_offset'`. Clears each qualifying open area a region holds, largest first, with full circles grown from its clearance seed — `r0, r0 + stepover, …` while `r <= R_max` — then records a **virtual island** so phase 2 is today's ring tree unchanged, with its first pass landing one stepover outside it. `seedIslandRadius` (issue #576) is what that radius is, and it is not the last centreline: the cutter following that lap removed stock out to `lastRadius + toolRadius`, so injecting the centreline under-states what phase 1 emptied by a whole tool radius and the tree spends its innermost levels threading slivers through metal that is already gone — 7-18% of the seeded stream's cut length was removing nothing measurable. The island is therefore extended by `min(toolRadius - stockToLeaveRadial, 2*toolRadius - stepover)` less one chord tolerance. The first term is the stock boundary the stack reached; the second is the cap that keeps the first ring outside the island still overlapping what the stack swept, and it only binds above a 50% stepover — without it a ridge opens all the way round the seed and costs more to walk out than the graze rings the extension removed. The epsilon is the chord tolerance and not a magic number: the island is a tessellated polygon whose chords already sit one sagitta inside the true circle, so subtracting the same tolerance errs toward a hairline air pass rather than a hairline uncut ridge. The island is **hard for offset generation and soft for cleanup entry** — its interior is stock the stack already removed, so `seedLeftover.ts` may plunge into it. Whatever the enlarged island strands, `seedLeftover.ts` recovers. An area must schedule at least three circles: one or two circles split and truncate the later offset tree without clearing enough middle to earn that handoff, so the largest such remainder ends phase 1 and regular rings clear it. When `roundLinkCorners` is enabled, the direct transition into each qualifying circle goes through the same domain-checked tangent-S splice as offset rings, re-seaming the arrival circle and starting the next concentric circle at that new seam; a rejected candidate retains the direct link. Multi-area is a loop over the same query: each pass hands the disc it emptied to the next **as an island**, which is what keeps "never clipped" true beyond the first area — a later area's circles stand in uncut material rather than being trimmed against a cleared one. `seedSeparation` is what that exclusion costs: one tool radius for the reach of the last circle plus two stepovers for the ring tree to grow a pass out of each side of the gap, because leaving less lets two expanded islands meet and collapses the region between them, and no ring is then emitted in a gap the cutter was too small to have cleared on its own. That charge is measured from `lastRadius` and the extension adds at most a tool radius, so two *extended* islands are still two stepovers apart — asserted in `seedClearing.test.ts` rather than left to the reader. The exclusion polygon is deliberately coarse and circumscribed — every later search measures against every polygon before it, so a chord-tolerance one would make planning quadratic in the number of areas for no benefit. Two properties of the surrounding code are what make phase 1 free of Clipper and free of per-circle containment tests, and both are load-bearing: the regions handed to the ring builder are already in **tool-centre space** and `buildInsetRegions` expands islands by exactly `stepover`, so the injected circle needs no radius conversion; and `findLargestClearanceCircle` is refined by branch-and-bound until the bound closes, so it **under-reports** `R_max` and never over-reports, making one query the whole schedule. Core coverage does not depend on where the entry helix actually landed — the first circle sweeps `[r0 - toolRadius, r0 + toolRadius]`, so any `r0 <= toolRadius` reaches its own centre, which is the single rule `seedStartRadius` exists to guarantee (helix entries use the orbit radius, plunge and ramp start at the tool radius). `pocket.ts` owns the handoff and the ordering: phase 1 runs for **every** qualifying open area in the band before phase 2 starts, never interleaved area by area, so all the open middles are gone before the first ring is cut (phase 2 then re-orders the trees from wherever phase 1 left the tool, and reproduces the previous ordering exactly when there are no seeds). It builds the tree from the region **with** the seed island and then restores the root's original island list, because `cutOffsetRegionNode` cuts every island of the node it is cutting and phase 1 has already run that exact lap — stripping it drops the duplicate and returns the seed disc to the cleared domain that entry placement and tangential links are tested against. It also owns the leftover excursions: `planRegionSeedLeftovers`, `seedLeftoverCandidates` and `cutSeedLeftoverExcursions` live there, planned once per band with the tree and cut after the rings at every level
- `boxedLoop.ts` — a closed tool-centre loop with its bounds and a row-bucketed edge index (issue #923): `pointInBoxedLoop` and `pointOnBoxedLoopEdge` give bit-for-bit the answers of the full ray-cast and on-edge walks the S-link and XY-lead domain gates used, reading only the edges whose Y span can affect the sample; probe counter counts edges scanned

- `modelSection.ts` — shared transformed imported-model section resolver. - `constantScallopField.ts` — the geodesic distance field and level-set extraction behind `finishSurfaceConstantScallop.ts`. Multi-source Dijkstra over the cutter-location grid with **true 3D edge weights** (`hypot(cellSize * |offset|, dz)`), on a **16-neighbour** stencil that adds the four knight moves to the usual 8: grid Dijkstra can only travel along its stencil directions, so it over-estimates Euclidean distance off-axis, and measured against a point source on a flat field that anisotropy is 7.10 % worst-case at 23 degrees with 8 neighbours and 2.02 % at 13 degrees with 16. That error lands straight on the pass spacing, so it is the difference between a 20 um cusp budget being held and being missed by a third. Every valid cell adjacent to an invalid one or to the grid edge is a seed, so a hole and a disconnected component each seed themselves rather than inheriting a distance across ground the cutter cannot reach. The binary heap breaks equal distances on cell index, which is what makes the field — and therefore the posted G-code — identical run to run. Level sets come out by marching squares at integer multiples of the spacing over the cell-**centre** lattice, with the two saddle cases resolved by the corner average so the two segments never cross; segments are joined on a rounded point key, open fragments are walked before closed ones so an open chain is never consumed as part of a loop, and contours are sorted on (level, minY, minX, closed) for a stable emission order
- `modelProtection.ts` — keeps cuts from violating the imported model; also owns `clampKeepOutPaths` / `clampKeepOuts`, the single definition of where a clamp forbids cutting, which every generator subtracts from its domain (issue #458)
- `clamps.ts` — the post-generation clamp backstop (`applyClampWarnings`: rapid auto-lift, crossing warnings, travel-limit) and `expandedClampBounds`, the checked footprint the 2D canvas and 3D viewport draw so the picture and the check cannot disagree
- `appendAll.ts` — `appendAll(target, source)`, the one way this folder splices a generated array onto another (issue #668). `target.push(...source)` passes one *argument* per element and throws `RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` past the engine's argument limit — 124,413 on node v26.0.0, stack-size dependent — and generation routinely builds arrays past that: one trochoidal Edge Route fragment on a 60 x 40 in guide emits 204,696 cut moves, well inside the operation point budget and well past the limit. A scoped `no-restricted-syntax` rule in `eslint.config.js` keeps the spread from coming back here

- `seedLeftover.ts` — the leftover probe behind the enlarged virtual island (issue #576). Extending the island is what deletes the graze rings and also what can strand stock, where the island merges with a wall or a real island and the ring tree collapses over a partial ring that would have cleared reachable metal. ## Tool compatibility (issue #955)

Generators and rest calculations resolve tools through `src/toolPolicy.ts` so milling refuses plasma consumables, including hand-edited references. `geometry.ts` normalizes plasma kerf to half-width radius and carries torch heights, delay and the drop (plunge) feed — a plasma tool's `defaultPlungeFeed` is the G-code pierce drop, so normalization converts it instead of zeroing it (#983) — while ignoring the milling-only defaults. `src/plasmaTool.test.ts` exercises every generator, using the real cone fixture for model operations.

- `plasma.ts` / `plasmaGeometry.ts` — experimental closed-contour plasma profiles: independent half-kerf offsets, physical swirl direction, scrap-side arc/line/centre lead fallback and exact neighbour kerf clearance on Top setups only; `plasma.test.ts` pins the real export direction, independent safety boundaries, conservative cache, units and persistence. Generation runs in three steps (issue #958): every target contour is resolved to its finished cut in selection order, so per-contour warnings keep their order; the cuts are ordered; the moves are written. Ordering changes the sequence of per-contour blocks and the rapids between them, never a block. `plasmaCutOrderInputs` is the one statement of what the order reads beyond the operation's own row and the features (the machine origin and the later plasma operations), shared with the toolpath cache.

- `plasmaOrder.ts` — the cut order of one plasma operation (issue #958), pure: points in, indices out. A containment tree of the operation's own target contours from their drawn rings (a container is strictly larger, candidates are tried smallest first behind a bounding-box test, so a nest of separate parts costs about one ring test per contour); cut children first, and one sibling subtree finished before the next, which at the top level is "part by part"; among siblings the nearest next pierce from where the previous cut ended, the first from the machine origin, with distances compared on the Clipper grid so rounding noise does not break a tie that the selection order should; and a preference for a rapid that does not pass over a piece already cut loose, the piece being left excepted. `outlinesEnclosingLaterCuts` is the geometry behind `plasmaOutlineBeforeHole`, the one case ordering inside an operation cannot fix: an outline cut here while a later plasma operation of the same setup cuts something inside it. `plasmaOrder.test.ts` pins each rule on hand-placed contours and, through the real generator, that every cut block equals the block its contour has when generated alone.

- `plasmaStartPoint.ts` — resolves optional contour-local start overrides through each instance transform, preserving move/rotate/scale attachment; focused pick/persistence/units coverage lives in `components/canvas/plasmaStartPoint.test.ts` and `plasmaStartPoint.test.ts`.

- `plasmaSetupIsTop` in `plasma.ts` checks the authoritative setup before the #946 dispatch transforms a Bottom project; both direct plasma generation and dispatch refuse non-Top plasma.
