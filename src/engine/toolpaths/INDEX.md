# INDEX — src/engine/toolpaths/

Toolpath generators. Each file owns one strategy. `index.ts` re-exports everything.

## Operations (per-strategy files)
- `pocket.ts` — pocket clearing (offset-based clearing of an enclosed area). Seeded pockets (issue #575) schedule offset work as a frontier of ring units with child-completion dependencies instead of draining a whole tree after its first selection: after every seed stack or offset unit the next entry is re-chosen globally from the actual endpoint, so seed stacks and offset branches interleave; links inside one tree stay direct (S-link eligible) and cross-section travel stays at safe Z. Unseeded pockets keep the legacy whole-tree sections, which is also the byte-identical fallback `seeded_offset` keeps when no seed fits. Also includes the `roundLinkCorners` tangential S-links (issue #545): the straight ring-to-ring link is replaced by an S-curve that departs ring N along its travel tangent and arrives on a vertex of ring N+1 along its travel tangent, with the arrival point free to slide along the next ring; when no S fits the cleared domain and budget, the straight link stays. The finish floor cuts the same ring set as the rough floor: per tree node both the outer contour and the island contours (`loops 'all'`), with island holes grown by `jtRound` when `roundOutsideCorners` is on (issue #550) — before this the finish skipped the island contours, leaving the corner lens between the rounded wall contour and the floor rings uncut (0.015495 in² of reachable stock on the repro; clear after). The finish floor also honours `pocketPattern: 'seeded_offset'` (issue #579): seeds are planned against each region the floor tree is built from, the last circle is injected as an island, and emission goes through the same #575 frontier scheduler as the rough pass — byte-identical to plain offset when no area schedules three circles; walls stay contour-based either way. `cleanWallCorners` acts on the finish pass's **wall contour** rather than its outermost floor ring (issue #622): the control is labelled "Round wall corners" and promises to round the ring that defines the wall, but it used to be handed to the floor tree, whose root sits one stepover inside the wall path — so the outermost floor ring gained arcs and cleanup loops while the wall contour itself stayed a sharp mitered rectangle in every variant. The wall block now runs the same `planContourSmoothing` + `buildWallCornerCleanupContour` (`cleanup: 'all'`) construction the rough wall ring uses, so the arc's lost coverage is put back by traversing the exact sharp span; `pocketFinishLeftover.test.ts` measures 0.000000 in² leftover with the control on. Output is byte-identical with the control off, which is its saved-project state (`cleanWallCorners` has no default and no `normalize` backfill). Feed reduction is applied **once per finish band level, over the whole level** (issue #622): the band emits a floor block and then a wall block at the same Z, and classifying only the floor left every wall-finish and wall-cleanup cut at full feed however engaged it was -- 15.428 in of unpriced cutting on the shipped `purecutcnc` example, including two 1.81 in full-width slots in a corridor one tool diameter wide in tool-centre space, where no floor ring fits and the wall and island contours are the only passes. Running the classifier from before the floor loop gives the wall block the floor's kerf as prior context, so an ordinary wall contour one stepover outside the outermost floor ring still keeps full feed; both classifiers walk the stream forward-only, so the floor block's own output is unchanged (asserted in `engagementPocket.test.ts`)
- `carving.ts` — engrave / carve along a path (`'direct'` centreline or `'trochoidal'` overlapping-orbit slot)
- `drilling.ts` — drill-cycle generation, plus V-bit countersinking (issue #489), which derives its plunge depth from the requested mouth diameter and deliberately emits expanded moves rather than a canned cycle
- `edge.ts` — edge / profile-following cuts (outside/inside contour)
- `vcarve.ts` — V-bit carving via inset-contour offset stepping (`v_carve`, "V-Carve offset")
- `vcarveMedial/` — geometric medial-axis v-carve (`v_carve_medial`): Voronoi-of-boundary skeleton with exact clearances, corner tips, and contact-spread curve filtering (see its `INDEX.md`). Replaced the retired `v_carve_recursive` skeleton op (issue #279); saved projects using it migrate to this on load.
- `roughSurface.ts` — 3D rough clearing of an imported mesh. Dispatches on the declared pattern table like the other clearing kinds (issue #618): `offset`/`seeded_offset` cut concentric rings (seeded runs phase 1's seed stacks first, then rings offset around each extended seed island with the root's own island list restored), `parallel` cuts the level boundary contours first, then `buildPocketParallelSegments` scanlines — the pocket and surface_clean raster branches cut the same contours before their segments, and without them the silhouette keeps a scalloped ridge of standing stock that offset's outermost ring removes. Every branch keeps the per-level model protection: entries synthesize against the level's own regions at the global safe Z (no `withEntryStartZ`), and any link that stays at depth must pass that level's `safeLinkCheck` or the transition retracts
- `finishSurface.ts` — 3D finish pass dispatcher
- `finishSurfaceCleanup.ts` — cleanup-style 3D imported-mesh finishing that emits deepest retained wall/floor paths from rough-surface-style levels. Its floor honours `pocketPattern: 'seeded_offset'` (issue #609): seeds are planned against the residual floor regions the passes are built from, and each plan's extended island is injected into `buildCleanupFloorOffsetPasses`'s **inset recursion only** — the depth-0 boundary contours still come from the region's own outer and islands, which is what keeps the `suppressedBoundarySegments` contract intact (the contours suppression is applied to are byte-for-byte the unseeded ones, so a seed can never reinstate a boundary pass the wall finishing removed). The #576 leftover probe runs per level after the circles and passes; without it this file's own fixture strands 95 of 15,897 swept-coverage samples the plain offset pattern clears. Byte-identical to `offset` when no area schedules three circles
- `finishSurfaceParallel.ts` — parallel-line finish strategy
- `finishSurfaceWaterline.ts` — waterline (constant-Z) finish strategy with bounded, user-tunable adaptive shallow-slope refinement, mesh-safe projected bands, and terminal peak coverage
- `surface.ts` — shared surface-toolpath helpers, and `surface_clean`'s own band generators. Its finish band mirrors the pocket's and carried the same issue #622 defect: `applyLevelFeed` ran at the end of the floor loop only, so the `wallStepLevels` block went out unclassified. Fixed the same way — one classification per band level
- `surfaceStepdown3d.ts` — shared imported-mesh stepdown resolver used by rough-surface and cleanup-surface operations
- `tabs.ts` — holding-tab generation on profile cuts
- `tabProfile.ts` — the pure Z profile a **smooth** tab imposes on one crossing: a normalized raised cosine with exact cut-Z at both footprint boundaries, exact `z_top` at the crossing centre, zero-slope joins at all three, and a sample count derived per crossing from a chord tolerance
- `tabSmoothing.ts` — chain-level smooth-tab motion. Measures each crossing in path distance along whole connected coplanar cut chains (rejoining a crossing that wraps a closed loop's seam) before sampling, so the emitted Z envelope depends on the crossing geometry rather than on how the source path was split into moves
- `trochoidalEdge.ts` — pure bounded overlapping-orbit sampling around a validated Edge Route guide; generation integration owns entry, fragments, and safety validation
- `multiFeature.ts` — ops that span multiple features (e.g. combined clearing)

## Supporting modules
- `index.ts` — barrel export — add new files here when adding a strategy
- `types.ts` — shared toolpath types (segments, passes, parameters)
- `pocketPatterns.ts` — the one source of truth for pocket-pattern dispatch (issue #609). `OPERATION_PATTERN_SUPPORT` declares, per `OperationKind`, both the patterns the dropdown **offers** and the pattern each generator **runs** for every member of `PocketPattern` — so the CAM panel's option list and the generators' branches cannot disagree, which is how the same defect shipped three times (#579, #583, #609; the third emitted no floor motion and no warning at all). Consumed by `CAMPanel.tsx`'s `pattern` renderer, `operationFields.ts`'s applicability, the operation booklet's pattern rows, and the five generators. The two `Record<>`s are the ratchet: a new operation kind does not compile until it says whether it takes a pattern, and a fifth `PocketPattern` does not compile until every pattern-taking kind classifies it and `areaCoverage`'s `never` default is satisfied. `offered` is declared rather than derived because the two sets genuinely differ — a pocket resolves a stored `waterline` to its offset rings without offering waterline, and cleanup leaves a stored `waterline` inert — but every offered pattern must resolve to one that cuts. `rough_surface` joined the clearing set in #618: its stored `waterline` maps to the rings the kind hard-coded before it had a pattern row at all
- `clearingControls.ts` — the one source of truth for which clearing controls each level-based kind offers, with the recorded reason for every cell that does not (issue #616). `CLEARING_CONTROL_SUPPORT` declares, per `OperationKind`, either the six cells (`slotFeed`, `engagementMode`, `roundOutsideCorners`, `cleanWallCorners`, `cornerRelief`, `machiningOrder`) or the reason the kind clears nothing — same two-`Record<>` ratchet as the pattern table: a new kind and a new control each fail to compile until classified. Consumed by `operationFields.ts`'s predicates, `pocket.ts`'s `resolveSlotFeedScale`, and the operation booklet's setting rows. Two boundaries are deliberate: tangential S-links stay with `usesTangentLinks` above (pattern-dependent, already single-owner), and the edge routes' shared rounding/relief/order half stays inline at each consumer because they are not clearing kinds and sit outside #614's scope. The structural test in `operationFields.test.ts` fails when a kind's exposed set diverges from the declaration
- `feed.ts` — shared `effectiveFeed` helper: applies a move's `feedScale` (for slot-feed pocket cuts) to the cut feed, and returns the plunge feed unmodified for plunge moves; used by the postprocessor, booklet time estimator, simulation playback, and the live-feed readout
- `engagement.ts` — pure cutter-engagement estimator (issue #498): `SweptMaterialIndex` models each prior sweep as an exact tool-radius capsule in a centreline-supercover spatial index, and returns the leading-semicircle measure in uncut material in `[0, π]` through closed-form capsule-arc unions; `nominalEngagement` gives the analytic stepover wrap angle; `engagementFeedScale` maps engagement onto a fixed non-uniform rung ladder — fine near full feed, coarse near slot (issue #591; rounding down, conservative), and `EngagementFeedQuantizer` applies hysteresis plus a minimum fragment length so `feedScale` changes stay controller- and arc-fitting-friendly — a rise climbing more than one rung holds only a quarter of that length, and a sub-minimum stretch is priced at its longer neighbour's rung rather than always dragging the pair down (issue #594, bounded to one rung above its own verdict and to one minimum fragment of distance); `EngagementTelemetryAccumulator` folds distance-weighted samples into per-operation max/p95/distance-above-nominal telemetry. Degenerate input resolves toward more engagement. Tests: `engagement.test.ts`, `engagementOracle.test.ts`, `engagementPocket.test.ts`
- `entry.ts` — clearance-aware plunge, helical, and zig-zag ramp entry synthesis for pocket, surface-clean, and rough-surface clearing operations, including deterministic fallback warnings and plunge-feed limiting
- `geometry.ts` — toolpath-specific geometric helpers; owns the shared `DEFAULT_FLATTEN_*` sampling constants
- `trochoidalPath.ts` — shared trochoidal entry synthesis (helix/plunge points, move-count budgeting, and the per-operation point budget type) used by both Edge Route and Engrave
- `trochoidalLevelPaths.ts` — one generated orbit path shared by every Z level whose guide fragments the same way (issue #661). A deep cut with no tabs and no obstacles fragments identically at every level, so the generator ran once per level and stored a copy each time; the store keys on the **planner's output geometry** — the guide points plus the four generator parameters — rather than on the causes of fragmentation (tabs, obstacles, region masks), because `buildTrochoidalContour` is a pure function of exactly those and a key derived from causes has to be extended by hand every time a new input to fragmentation lands. Identical key therefore means provably identical output, and anything that changed the fragmentation is a different key by construction. Carried on `TrochoidalOperationBudget`, which #661 split into two accounts: `remainingMoves` charges every level, carries the pre-#661 arithmetic and is what refuses; `remainingPoints` charges a generated path once. Separating them is what lets #662 raise the emission ceiling without multiplying generation cost — charging generation *alone* took a 60 x 40 in outside route from an honest `edgeTrochoidalMoveBudget` refusal to 9,873,183 moves and ~2 GB of heap, because a reused array of points still describes motion the machine cuts at every level. **Sharing the path never shares the check**: the verification backstop and entry synthesis still run for every level at that level's own Z — `edge.ts` is where that is enforced and `trochoidalLevelSharing.test.ts` is what holds it there. A refused build is never cached (`move-budget` is a function of the remaining budget, not of the guide)
- `guideFragments.ts` — cyclic closed-guide fragmentation against pre-unioned keep-outs; preserves every safe span before entry-bearing motion is generated
- `tabs.ts` also owns the shared tab footprint geometry (`expandedTabFootprints`) and `applyEdgeRouteTabs`, the edge-route tab pass that deliberately returns trochoidal results untouched — see `planning/TROCHOIDAL_EDGE_DESIGN.md`
- `cornerRelief.ts` — corner relief (dogbone / T-bone / longest edge, `operation.cornerRelief`). Detects the convex corners of whatever region an operation clears — one sign test against which side of each loop the cleared material lies on, so the module never learns which operation kind called it — and emits relief as a **separate stepped pass appended after the main path**, not as a region modification (which silently no-ops: a radius-`r` lobe eroded by a radius-`r` cutter collapses to a discarded point) and not as an inline wall detour (which becomes a full-depth slot under the rough-big/finish-small workflow). The excursion ends where the cutter just *touches* the corner — the exact limit, not a conservative one — and the pass descends on the operation's own tool-centre path corner, which it does only when the emitted moves prove the operation cut there at or below the deepest relief level. That one guard covers `finishWalls`-off pockets, tab-covered outside corners, and trochoidal channels that never reach the contour, without naming any of them. Levels come from `min(tool.defaultStepdown, tool.maxCutDepth)`, never from `operation.stepdown` (hidden and stale on a finish pass)
- `offsetSmoothing.ts` — emit-time corner smoothing for the outer/wall clearing rings; shared by pocket + surface clearing when `roundOutsideCorners` is enabled. `planContourSmoothing` is the contour-level turn planner (issue #546): it analyses the whole closed ring, groups consecutive same-sign turning vertices into one geometric turn run, allocates straight-edge setback at contour scope rather than capping every corner at half its shorter edge, and returns the rounded contour plus per-turn metadata (source span, entry/exit tangent points, effective radius). `roundContourCorners` / `smoothClosedContours` / `cornerSmoothingRadius` are the points-only wrappers. Fails closed to the exact source geometry per turn (a starved shoulder that would put a tangent point inside its own run) and per contour (self-intersection, non-finite values). A run whose *path* — vertices **and segment midpoints** — already tracks a circle at least as broad as the request is left untouched; measuring vertices alone is meaningless, since any three points fit a circle exactly and a square's corners are concyclic. With `broadCorners` on (opt-in; off is today's exact output for every other caller) a turn the ordinary construction starves — a corner reached only through short tessellation edges, which comes out at a few percent of the request — is instead cut with one arc at the *full* radius across as many source vertices as that takes, via `broadCornerArc.ts`. Such a transition reports `cutsAcrossSource` and carries `spanPoints`, the geometry it cut away with the ordinary fillet spliced back in; a caller may only enable the option if it cleans that span, because the arc leaves a tip of stock behind. Rough/surface-pass islands are rounded the opposite way — via `jtRound` Clipper offsets (see `buildInsetRegions` island join), so the tool wraps convex island corners smoothly without gouging; the pocket finish wall's islands now go through this planner too (see the `pocket.ts` entry).
- `broadCornerArc.ts` — the full-radius corner arc for tessellated approaches (issue #546): rolls a circle of the requested radius into the corner until it jams against two *real* source edges, so both junctions are tangent-continuous by construction. This is the difference from the reverted attempt that fitted an arc to two shoulder lines read from further out — the approach curves, those lines leave the polyline, and the arc met the path at a kink on each side. A candidate is rejected unless both tangent points land inside their own segments, the sweep equals the source path's own accumulated turn, and nothing of the ring lies inside the circle; that last pair is what makes a ring narrower than two radii (a pocket's innermost slivers) decline and keep the geometry it already had
- `sweptCoverage.ts` — "has some other pass already removed this?" (issue #546): tool centrelines indexed into a uniform grid at one radius per cell. `pathIsCovered` asks whether a *line* lies inside the swept envelope; `sweptRegionIsCovered` asks whether everything a cutter following that line would *remove* is already gone, by rasterising the path's disc. The distinction is the whole point and cost a shipped defect to learn: a corner cleanup loop was dropped because the span it retraces was covered, while the loop also sweeps stock either side of that span, leaving a 0.21 x 0.05in patch 0.0057in proud. The area test needs no region check — callers hand it a loop already validated against the tool-centre domain, so every cell its disc reaches is material by construction. Every unanswerable question answers *no*. Used by Pocket to drop the redundant cleanup loops (which is what makes rounding cheaper than not rounding) and by `pocketClearance.test.ts`
- `wallCornerCleanup.ts` — pairs a rounded corner with an immediate same-Z cleanup of the stock its arc left behind, so a ring can be rounded where rounding otherwise removes coverage (issue #546). `buildWallCornerCleanupContour` emits the broad transition, returns to the transition entry along a domain-checked cubic, then traverses the span, restoring the coverage. Which corners get a loop is the `cleanup` option: a wall ring cleans `'all'` of them and traverses the exact sharp source span, restoring the legacy wall coverage; an interior ring cleans only `'cut-across'` — the transitions that cut across their own source vertices — and traverses their `spanPoints`, which keep the ordinary fillet so the loop rejoins on a curve rather than putting the bare apex back into the path. Both the arc and the return are sampled against the Pocket tool-centre domain; a corner failing either keeps its exact sharp geometry, so one reflex corner (whose arc always bulges off the ring into the wall) costs only itself and not the whole ring. `cleanupCount` reports how many corners were actually cleaned — 0 with planned transitions is what raises `pocketWallCornerCleanupFallback`.
- `seedClearing.ts` — seeded circle pocket clearing, phase 1 (issue #554), reached by `pocketPattern: 'seeded_offset'`. Clears each qualifying open area a region holds, largest first, with full circles grown from its clearance seed — `r0, r0 + stepover, …` while `r <= R_max` — then records a **virtual island** so phase 2 is today's ring tree unchanged, with its first pass landing one stepover outside it. `seedIslandRadius` (issue #576) is what that radius is, and it is not the last centreline: the cutter following that lap removed stock out to `lastRadius + toolRadius`, so injecting the centreline under-states what phase 1 emptied by a whole tool radius and the tree spends its innermost levels threading slivers through metal that is already gone — 7-18% of the seeded stream's cut length was removing nothing measurable. The island is therefore extended by `min(toolRadius - stockToLeaveRadial, 2*toolRadius - stepover)` less one chord tolerance. The first term is the stock boundary the stack reached; the second is the cap that keeps the first ring outside the island still overlapping what the stack swept, and it only binds above a 50% stepover — without it a ridge opens all the way round the seed and costs more to walk out than the graze rings the extension removed. The epsilon is the chord tolerance and not a magic number: the island is a tessellated polygon whose chords already sit one sagitta inside the true circle, so subtracting the same tolerance errs toward a hairline air pass rather than a hairline uncut ridge. The island is **hard for offset generation and soft for cleanup entry** — its interior is stock the stack already removed, so `seedLeftover.ts` may plunge into it. Whatever the enlarged island strands, `seedLeftover.ts` recovers. An area must schedule at least three circles: one or two circles split and truncate the later offset tree without clearing enough middle to earn that handoff, so the largest such remainder ends phase 1 and regular rings clear it. When `roundLinkCorners` is enabled, the direct transition into each qualifying circle goes through the same domain-checked tangent-S splice as offset rings, re-seaming the arrival circle and starting the next concentric circle at that new seam; a rejected candidate retains the direct link. Multi-area is a loop over the same query: each pass hands the disc it emptied to the next **as an island**, which is what keeps "never clipped" true beyond the first area — a later area's circles stand in uncut material rather than being trimmed against a cleared one. `seedSeparation` is what that exclusion costs: one tool radius for the reach of the last circle plus two stepovers for the ring tree to grow a pass out of each side of the gap, because leaving less lets two expanded islands meet and collapses the region between them, and no ring is then emitted in a gap the cutter was too small to have cleared on its own. That charge is measured from `lastRadius` and the extension adds at most a tool radius, so two *extended* islands are still two stepovers apart — asserted in `seedClearing.test.ts` rather than left to the reader. The exclusion polygon is deliberately coarse and circumscribed — every later search measures against every polygon before it, so a chord-tolerance one would make planning quadratic in the number of areas for no benefit. Two properties of the surrounding code are what make phase 1 free of Clipper and free of per-circle containment tests, and both are load-bearing: the regions handed to the ring builder are already in **tool-centre space** and `buildInsetRegions` expands islands by exactly `stepover`, so the injected circle needs no radius conversion; and `findLargestClearanceCircle` is refined by branch-and-bound until the bound closes, so it **under-reports** `R_max` and never over-reports, making one query the whole schedule. Core coverage does not depend on where the entry helix actually landed — the first circle sweeps `[r0 - toolRadius, r0 + toolRadius]`, so any `r0 <= toolRadius` reaches its own centre, which is the single rule `seedStartRadius` exists to guarantee (helix entries use the orbit radius, plunge and ramp start at the tool radius). `pocket.ts` owns the handoff and the ordering: phase 1 runs for **every** qualifying open area in the band before phase 2 starts, never interleaved area by area, so all the open middles are gone before the first ring is cut (phase 2 then re-orders the trees from wherever phase 1 left the tool, and reproduces the previous ordering exactly when there are no seeds). It builds the tree from the region **with** the seed island and then restores the root's original island list, because `cutOffsetRegionNode` cuts every island of the node it is cutting and phase 1 has already run that exact lap — stripping it drops the duplicate and returns the seed disc to the cleared domain that entry placement and tangential links are tested against. It also owns the leftover excursions: `planRegionSeedLeftovers`, `seedLeftoverCandidates` and `cutSeedLeftoverExcursions` live there, planned once per band with the tree and cut after the rings at every level
- `tangentLink.ts` — tangential ring-to-ring S-links (issue #545): `tangentSLink` builds the arc-line-arc path that departs the exit point along the previous ring's tangent and arrives on a vertex of the directed arrival ring along its tangent (the arrival vertex slides, the middle direction is swept, one arc radius is pinned to the floor, the closure solves the rest); `buildOffsetDomainCheck` is the cleared-domain predicate and `pocketTangentLinkOptions` the gate options (minRadius 0.25×D, length budget 2.5×D). No candidate fits ⇒ the straight link stays
- `linearMoveOptimization.ts` — pure generation-stage finalizer that removes zero-length duplicate moves and merges contiguous, direction-preserving, collinear XY moves; applied after tabs but before clamp warnings. Zero-length **rapids** are kept: they are each operation's entry-positioning marker for the postprocessor, not noise
- `arcReconstruction.ts` — recovers arcs/circles/beziers from flattened Clipper output: known-circle reconstruction, segment-preserving boolean reconstruction (annotation map), and the Clipper-offset simplification pipeline (Kasa fit + RDP)
- `regions.ts` — region computation (which area belongs to which op): `buildRegionMask` composes region features into an ordered include/exclude mask in feature-tree order
- `regionDomain.ts` — typed region-mask → domain resolvers (`resolveRegionDomainArea` / `resolveRegionDomainCentre` / `resolveRegionDomainCurve`) shared by pocket, edge, carving, v-carve, surface, rest-region, parallel, and waterline finishing; the resolver table in `planning/REGION_FEATURE_SEMANTICS.md` owns which operation kind uses which resolver — do not re-implement mask composition per operation (issue #476)
- `toolpathDependencies.ts` — pure change-detection for the per-operation toolpath cache (issue #518, slice S1): `featureInstanceComputationEquals` (resolver-read instance fields only) and `diffToolpathInputs` (changed feature ids plus global invalidation for named-dimension, unit, model-asset, and feature-order changes). Not wired into `isCacheHit` yet — slice S2 owns that seam.
- `resolver.ts` — resolves features+operations into clipper input regions; V-carve accepts closed Subtract and Line features (S2), Pocket remains Subtract-only; Line paths use even-odd fill semantics for nested contour holes
- `restRegions.ts` — rest-machining region computation (what a prior tool missed)
- `silhouette.ts` — extracts 2D silhouette from 3D mesh for sketch projection
- `meshSlicing.ts` — slices a mesh at Z heights (used by surface strategies)
- `modelProtection.ts` — keeps cuts from violating the imported model
- `clamps.ts` — clamp clearance / avoidance regions

## Tests
- `cornerRelief.test.ts` — corner relief asserted on **coverage of the emitted motion**, never on "the region changed": a swept-envelope model decides whether the corner wedge is cut, because a region-level assertion passes on the silent no-op that region union produces. The same model is run with the excursion ending at `1.2r` and must *fail*, which is what makes the touch-the-corner rule falsifiable rather than merely satisfied. Also covers the corner-qualification sets (convex boundary, reflex island, concave part outline), tessellated arcs and fillets producing nothing, `'none'`/legacy identity, the tool-derived stepdown, every guard path, the rough-big-then-finish-small workflow, `optimizeLinearMoves` preserving the out-and-back (it survives only because the collinear merge requires `dot > 0`), and mm/inch plus a cross-unit tool
- `linearMoveOptimization.test.ts` — zero-length removal, entry-marker (zero-length rapid) preservation, collinear merge, and boundary preservation
- `pocketClearance.test.ts` — corner smoothing may not leave stock (issue #546). Rasterises the material and tests every cell against the swept envelope of the emitted cuts, against a self-referential bar: rounding may not leave more uncleared material than not rounding, so whatever a round cutter can never enter cancels out. No other test here can see an option that quietly stops removing material, which is the one failure that reaches the workpiece. `pocket-rounded-corner-coverage.camj` is the fixture that caught a cleanup loop being dropped while it was still the only pass clearing part of the floor; three other pocket fixtures showed nothing
- `pocketFinishLeftover.test.ts` — the finish pass may not leave reachable island-corner stock (issue #550). Measures geometrically (morphological opening minus the swept envelope, via `src/test/pocketLeftover.ts`) on `pocket-finish-island-leftover.camj`: the rounded pass must come back clear (≤ 0.0002 in² vs the 0.015495 baseline — the defect was a missing island ring, not corner treatment), every emitted cut must clear every island by the finish tool radius (no pass may gouge), the unrounded pass must clear too (the missing ring, not the corner treatment, was the defect), and the island-free control must come back clear
- `sweptCoverage.test.ts` — the swept-envelope test (issue #546): the band boundary at exactly one radius, grid placement for far and negative coordinates and across cell rows, an excursion in the *interior* of a segment (both endpoints inside the band, the middle outside — only sampling finds it), and every fail-closed input
- `broadCornerArc.test.ts` — the full-radius corner search (issue #546): tangency to real source edges inside their own segments, the jam on a tessellated corner whose adjacent edge is a fraction of the request, walking further out when a short stub cannot hold the tangent point, declining a corridor narrower than two radii and a circle blocked by the far wall, sweep matching the source turn, scale/orientation equivalence, determinism, and an invariant fuzz over deterministically perturbed rings (never `Math.random`) asserting all of the above on every arc it returns
- `seedClearing.test.ts` — the two claims that reach the workpiece, plus the ones that let it ship. **No circle is ever clipped**: every emitted point tested against the region and its islands. **The seed disc is fully cleared**: rasterised against the swept envelope of the circles alone rather than inferred from the schedule, and re-run with the first radius pushed past the tool radius where it must *fail* — that falsification is what makes `r0 <= toolRadius` a tested rule instead of a comment. **Fewer than three circles ⇒ byte-identical to `offset`**, asserted move-for-move on a 653-move stream through real generation (a 20 mm pocket with a 6 mm tool leaves a 14 mm tool-centre region, enough for exactly two circles); a direct schedule fixture keeps the exact three-circle boundary. **Seed-circle S-links** compare enabled/disabled real streams, require direct radial links to be replaced by tessellated tangent paths, and rasterise the two swept envelopes to prove the replacement drops no coverage. **Several open areas in one region** each get their own seed, on a dumbbell whose two lobes are joined by a narrower neck: largest first, the second landing in the *other* lobe, and every later area's circles provably clear of every disc an earlier one emptied. **Phase 1 precedes phase 2 across areas**, on a two-pocket fixture, with phase-2 motion identified positively — a cut outside every seed's handoff radius can be neither a circle nor one of the radial links between circles. Also: the schedule stops at the first radius that does not fit, tessellation whose points lie exactly on the circle within the chord tolerance, the handoff sitting one stepover out, the seed cut once and not twice, and an island region neither seeding into the island nor crossing it
- `seedLeftover.ts` — the leftover probe behind the enlarged virtual island (issue #576). Extending the island is what deletes the graze rings and also what can strand stock, where the island merges with a wall or a real island and the ring tree collapses over a partial ring that would have cleared reachable metal. The recovery is deliberately not new geometry: **candidates are the contours of the tree built around the PRE-extension island** — the shipped behaviour — so every excursion is a *subpath of a path this code already emits*, and "cannot gouge" needs no fresh argument. A candidate vertex is needed when the disc a cutter standing on it would remove is not already inside the emitted swept envelope (`sweptCoverage.ts`, issue #546); everything in that disc is removable stock by construction, because the domain is the pocket already inset by `toolRadius + radialLeave`. Maximal runs of needed vertices become open excursions extended one vertex each side — that neighbour is *not* needed, so its centre sits in swept material and the excursion plunges through metal a previous pass removed, no helix or ramp budget. Only a candidate loop needed end to end is emitted closed, through the level's ordinary entry. Two constants carry the accuracy: candidates are resampled to `toolRadius/2` before probing (a mitered ring vertex can be 5 mm from its neighbour, which rounds a 6 mm sliver up to a 15 mm excursion), and the disc is sampled at `toolRadius/12` — a sixth resolves the wall ridges but leaves pinpoint cusps where an excursion's envelope meets a ring's. Each excursion is folded into the coverage before the next candidate is judged, or every level of the pre-extension tree emits its own pass over the same ridge. The coverage it judges against is built from the **smoothed** ring contours when rounding is on: raw contours claim the corner tips the emitter cuts short, which was worth six stranded samples on a rounded 70x50 pocket. Runs once per region, never per step level, and not at all when no island got an extension. Emitted after the level's rings, and only after — "already swept" is measured at that Z, which is what makes the plunge land in a slot rather than a stepdown of stock
